\section{Introduction}

This document describes the three-channel visual feedback logic of the
Interface~A Control Group condition in the CDI-PRBF (Personalised Resonance
Biofeedback) system. Interface~A adopts an \textit{abstract-instrumental}
representational style: a pulsing circle whose size, colour, and ambient
particle field each convey one independent dimension of the user's
physiological state.

In the Control Group condition, the breathing frequency is fixed at 0.1~Hz
(6~bpm), and the subject follows the on-screen pacing while physiological
signals drive real-time colour and reward feedback.

The three feedback channels are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Circle size} — breathing-guidance pacing (an exogenously
    generated signal, independent of physiology).
  \item \textbf{Circle colour} — real-time HRV entrainment score, measuring
    how much HRV power is concentrated at the guidance breathing frequency.
  \item \textbf{Particle burst} — a discrete reward event triggered when LF
    (low-frequency) heart-rate-variability power exceeds a threshold,
    signalling strong resonance.
\end{enumerate}

Each channel is described below in terms of its \textit{input signal},
\textit{transform}, and \textit{visual mapping}.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Channel 1: Circle Size — Breathing Pacing}

\subsection{Input}
The pacing engine prescribes a target breathing frequency $f_{\text{bpm}} = 6.0$
breaths per minute (0.1~Hz) and a symmetrical inhale-to-exhale ratio $R_I:R_E
= 5:5$ (configurable).

\subsection{Transform}
Let the total cycle duration be
\[
T_{\text{cycle}} = \frac{60}{f_{\text{bpm}}} \quad\text{(seconds)}.
\]

The inhale and exhale segment durations are
\[
T_I = T_{\text{cycle}} \cdot \frac{R_I}{R_I + R_E}, \qquad
T_E = T_{\text{cycle}} \cdot \frac{R_E}{R_I + R_E}.
\]

Within each segment, phase progress $p \in [0,1]$ is defined as
\[
p(t) = \frac{t - t_{\text{seg-onset}}}{T_{\text{seg}}},
\]
clamped to $[0,1]$. The raw progress is reshaped by a quadratic ease-in-out
function to avoid linear-motion artefacts:
\[
\text{ease}(p) =
\begin{cases}
2p^2,                & p < 0.5, \\
-1 + (4 - 2p) \cdot p, & p \geq 0.5.
\end{cases}
\]

\subsection{Visual Mapping}
The circle's CSS \texttt{transform: scale} is linearly interpolated between
two anchor scales:
\[
S_{\text{inhale}} = 1.42, \qquad S_{\text{exhale}} = 0.72.
\]

During inhale, the circle expands:
\[
s(t) = (1 - \text{ease}(p)) \cdot S_{\text{exhale}} \;+\; \text{ease}(p) \cdot S_{\text{inhale}}.
\]

During exhale, the circle contracts:
\[
s(t) = (1 - \text{ease}(p)) \cdot S_{\text{inhale}} \;+\; \text{ease}(p) \cdot S_{\text{exhale}}.
\]

A concentric arc simultaneously drains clockwise, completing one full
circumference per cycle, providing a secondary spatial cue for cycle progress.

\paragraph{Rationale.}
The dynamic range $[0.72, 1.42]$ was chosen such that the circle is never
smaller than~72\,\% nor larger than~142\,\% of its rest size, keeping the
visual anchor recognisable across the full cycle. Quadratic easing mimics
natural diaphragmatic breathing kinematics (slow start/end, faster mid-phase),
which pilot testing suggested yields smoother perceived pacing than linear
interpolation.

\section{Channel 2: Circle Colour — Frequency-Domain Entrainment}

When the breathing frequency is held at 0.1~Hz, colour becomes a
physiologically driven channel. The system continuously estimates how much
of the user's HRV power is concentrated at the guidance frequency, yielding a
real-time \textit{entrainment score}. This frequency-domain approach is more
robust than time-domain cross-correlation for short (60~s) windows because it
does not require explicit phase alignment between HR and the breathing
reference.

\subsubsection{Signal Chain}

Let the unevenly sampled heart-rate time series be
$\{(t_i, h_i)\}_{i=1}^{M}$, where $t_i$ is the timestamp (seconds) and $h_i$
is the instantaneous heart rate (BPM). A sliding window of $W = 60$~s is
maintained; samples with $t_i < t_{\text{now}} - W$ are discarded.

\paragraph{Step 1 — Resampling.}
The unevenly sampled HR series is linearly interpolated onto an even time grid
with sampling rate $f_s = 4$~Hz, yielding a discrete signal
$\mathbf{x} = \{x_0, \dots, x_{N-1}\}$ where $N = \lfloor W \cdot f_s \rfloor$.
The DC component is removed:
\[
\tilde{x}_n = x_n - \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1} x_j.
\]

\paragraph{Step 2 — Welch PSD.}
Rather than a single-window DFT (which yields a noisy spectrum for short
recordings), Welch's method is used to obtain a low-variance power spectral
density estimate. The zero-mean signal $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}$ of length $N$ is
divided into $K$ overlapping segments of length $L = 128$, with hop size
$H = 32$ (75\,\% overlap). Each segment $m$ is multiplied by a Hanning window
$\mathbf{w}$ to suppress sidelobe leakage:
\[
x_n^{(m)} = \tilde{x}_{n + mH} \cdot w_n, \qquad
w_n = 0.5\left(1 - \cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi n}{L-1}\right)\right), \quad n = 0, \dots, L-1.
\]

Each windowed segment is zero-padded to $N_{\text{FFT}} = 256$ (the next power
of two) and transformed via radix-2 Cooley--Tukey FFT, yielding the periodogram
$|X_m[k]|^2$ for segment $m$. The Welch PSD estimate is the segment-averaged
periodogram:
\[
\hat{P}[k] = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{m=0}^{K-1} |X_m[k]|^2, \qquad
k = 0, \dots, N_{\text{FFT}}/2.
\]

The band power is the sum of the averaged PSD over the guidance band
$\mathcal{B} = [f_g - \Delta, f_g + \Delta]$ with $\Delta = 0.02$~Hz:
\[
P_{\text{band}} = \sum_{k:\; f_k \in \mathcal{B}} \hat{P}[k], \qquad
f_k = \frac{k \cdot f_s}{N_{\text{FFT}}}.
\]

Note that because the entrainment score is a \textit{ratio} of powers, the
absolute PSD normalisation (coherent gain of the Hanning window) cancels out
and can be omitted from the computation.

\paragraph{Step 3 — Total Power.}
By Parseval's theorem, total signal power equals the zero-mean variance:
\[
P_{\text{total}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \tilde{x}_n^{\,2}.
\]

\paragraph{Step 4 — Entrainment Score.}
The raw entrainment score is the power ratio, clamped to $[0, 1]$:
\[
S_{\text{raw}} = \min\!\left(1,\; \max\!\left(0,\; \frac{P_{\text{band}}}{P_{\text{total}}}\right)\right).
\]

An entrainment score of~1.0 indicates that virtually all HRV power is
concentrated at (and immediately around) the guidance breathing frequency;
a score near~0 indicates that HR variability is dominated by other
frequencies (or absent altogether).

\subsubsection{Temporal Smoothing}

Raw entrainment score is smoothed by exponential moving average (EMA) with
blend factor $\alpha = 0.3$:
\[
S_{\text{smooth}}^{(t)} = \alpha \cdot S_{\text{raw}}^{(t)}
                         + (1 - \alpha) \cdot S_{\text{smooth}}^{(t-1)}.
\]

\subsubsection{Colour-Level Mapping with Hysteresis}

The smoothed coherence is discretised into four colour levels with
hysteresis to suppress flickering:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Level} & \textbf{Coherence Range} & \textbf{Colour} \\
\midrule
0 & $C < 0.4$                  & White \quad \texttt{rgb(255,255,255)} \\
1 & $0.4 \leq C < 0.6$        & Light Blue \quad \texttt{rgb(179,217,255)} \\
2 & $0.6 \leq C < 0.8$        & Sky Blue \quad \texttt{rgb(102,179,255)} \\
3 & $C \geq 0.8$              & Deep Blue \quad \texttt{rgb(26,128,230)} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Transitions between levels require \textit{streak confirmation}: three
consecutive coherence values must lie in the candidate level's range
($\pm 0.1$ hysteresis band) before the colour changes. A 60-second
calibration delay after session start suppresses discolouration during
the initial settling period.

\subsubsection{Visual Rendering}

The mapped colour is applied simultaneously to: the circle's radial
gradient (lighter at top-left, fading to dark navy at the rim), an outer
ring stroke, the concentric arc, and the phase-text label. The circle
also carries a CSS \texttt{box-shadow} glow in the same colour. This
\textit{multi-element colour binding} ensures the chromatic signal is
perceptible even in peripheral vision.

\paragraph{Rationale.}
The frequency-domain approach was chosen over time-domain cross-correlation for
three reasons. First, for short windows ($\leq 60$~s, containing only 6
breathing cycles at 0.1~Hz), Welch PSD provides a low-variance spectral
estimate — segment averaging with 75\,\% overlap and Hanning windowing
suppresses both spectral leakage and estimation variance, making the band-power
ratio more stable than a single-window DFT or cross-correlation with phase
alignment. Second, the power-ratio formulation naturally handles the case where
HRV is weak overall (both numerator and denominator are small, producing a low
score) as well as the case where HRV is strong but at the wrong frequency
(large denominator, small numerator — also a low score). Third, with $K \approx
4$ segments of $L = 128$ points and $N_{\text{FFT}} = 256$, the Welch
computation ($\sim$4~radix-2 FFTs) runs comfortably in a browser animation
frame and does not require the breathing phase signal — only the guidance
frequency is needed.

The four-level discretisation with hysteresis was chosen over a continuous
colour ramp because pilot participants reported that continuously morphing hues
were difficult to interpret as \textit{states}; the stepped palette with streak
confirmation provides clear, stable feedback about whether resonance quality is
improving, stable, or degrading. The calibration delay prevents early erratic
readings from anchoring an incorrect baseline impression.

\section{Channel 3: Particle Burst — LF-Power Reward}

\subsection{Input}
LF power (ms\textsuperscript{2}) is computed by the backend HRV analyser from
a sliding window of inter-beat intervals and streamed to the frontend at
approximately 1~Hz. LF power in the 0.04--0.15~Hz band is the primary HRV
marker of baroreflex-mediated resonance~\cite{lehrer2014}.

\subsection{Trigger Logic}

A discrete particle burst (``blossom'') fires when \textit{all} of the
following conditions are satisfied:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Threshold:} $\text{LF} \geq \tau$, where default
    $\tau = 500$~ms\textsuperscript{2} (user-configurable).
  \item \textbf{Streak:} Three consecutive readings above threshold
    (confirms the elevation is sustained, not a single-sample artefact).
  \item \textbf{Cooldown:} At least $T_{\text{cd}} = 120$~seconds since
    the previous burst.
  \item \textbf{Cap:} Fewer than $M = 5$ bursts have occurred in the
    current session.
\end{enumerate}

When all conditions hold, a burst is emitted and the streak counter resets.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Flower Blossom Decision Logic (per analysis cycle, $\sim$1~Hz)}
\label{alg:flower-blossom}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $P_{\text{LF}}$, $\theta_{\text{LF}} = 500$~ms$^2$, $N_{\text{req}} = 3$, $T_{\text{cool}} = 120$~s, $N_{\max} = 5$
\Ensure Blossom trigger or not
\If{session inactive or current blossoms $\geq N_{\max}$}
  \State \Return
\EndIf
\If{time since last blossom $< T_{\text{cool}}$}
  \State \Return
\EndIf
\If{$P_{\text{LF}} \geq \theta_{\text{LF}}$}
  \State $\text{streak} \leftarrow \text{streak} + 1$
  \If{$\text{streak} \geq N_{\text{req}}$}
    \State TriggerBlossom(\textit{blossomIndex})
    \State $\text{streak} \leftarrow 0$
    \State $\text{blossomCount} \leftarrow \text{blossomCount} + 1$
  \EndIf
\Else
  \State $\text{streak} \leftarrow 0$
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Progress Accumulator}

Between bursts, a continuous progress value $P \in [0,1]$ is computed as
\[
P = \min\!\left(1,\; \frac{t - t_{\text{last}}}{T_{\text{cd}}}\right)
      \cdot \left(0.5 + 0.5 \cdot \min\!\left(1,\; \frac{\text{LF}}{2\tau}\right)\right),
\]
where $t$ is the current session time and $t_{\text{last}}$ is the timestamp
of the most recent burst. The first factor models cooldown recovery; the second
factor modulates the rate by current LF power, so that higher LF power fills
the progress indicator faster (up to $2\times$ the baseline rate).

\subsection{Particle Animation}

Each burst spawns $N = 100$ particles from the screen centre. Each particle
$j$ is initialised as:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Angle:} $\theta_j = \frac{2\pi j}{N} + \delta,\;
    \delta \sim \mathcal{U}(-0.3, 0.3)$ rad — near-uniform radial distribution
    with slight random jitter.
  \item \textbf{Speed:} $v_j \sim \mathcal{U}(120, 440)$ px/s.
  \item \textbf{Hue:} $H_j \sim \mathcal{U}(190, 270)$ — spanning cyan to
    purple in HSL colour space.
  \item \textbf{Life:} $L_j = 1.0$, decaying at
    $d_j \sim \mathcal{U}(0.4, 0.9)$ per second.
  \item \textbf{Size:} $r_j \sim \mathcal{U}(4, 12)$ px.
\end{itemize}

Particle dynamics follow a simple kinematic model with gravity:
\begin{align*}
  x_j(t+\Delta t) &= x_j(t) + v_{x,j} \cdot \Delta t, \\
  y_j(t+\Delta t) &= y_j(t) + v_{y,j} \cdot \Delta t, \\
  v_{y,j}(t+\Delta t) &= v_{y,j}(t) + 60 \cdot \Delta t \quad\text{(px/s\textsuperscript{2} downward)}.
\end{align*}

Rendering uses HSL colour with lightness proportional to remaining life
($55 + 20 \cdot L_j$), alpha faded by a factor of $1.2 \cdot L_j$, and a
12~px glow shadow. Particles are removed when $L_j \leq 0$; the animation
loop terminates when all particles are dead, clearing the overlay canvas.

\paragraph{Rationale.}
The burst is designed as a \textit{discrete positive reinforcer} for
achieving high LF power — a physiological state the user cannot directly
control but can influence through breathing quality. The three-condition
gate (threshold + streak + cooldown) prevents false triggering from
motion artefacts (which typically produce single-sample outliers) and
avoids satiation by limiting frequency (max $\approx 5$ per 10-minute
session). The particle aesthetic (cyan-to-purple with glow) was selected
to be visually distinct from the breathing circle's blue-green palette,
ensuring the reward event is perceptually salient without being
confusable with the continuous guidance channels.

\subsection{Persistent Particle Ring}

While the transient burst provides an immediate reward event, it
disappears within seconds and leaves no lasting trace of the user's
cumulative progress across the session. A \textit{persistent particle
ring} is therefore maintained around the breathing circle as a durable,
ambient progress indicator.

\subsubsection{Stage Model}

The ring has $M + 1 = 6$ discrete visual states ($0$ through $5$),
corresponding to the number of bursts earned so far in the session:

\[
\text{stage} = \text{blossomCount} \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}.
\]

Each stage adds a fixed set of particles to the ring, so that the visual
density grows monotonically with cumulative achievement. Stage~0 shows
an empty ring (no persistent particles); stage~5 shows the full
complement.

\subsubsection{Particle Allocation}

Let $Q$ be the total number of persistent particles at stage~5 (default
$Q = 60$). Particles are distributed evenly across stages, with the
number of particles active at stage $s$ given by

\[
N_{\text{persist}}(s) = \left\lfloor \frac{s}{M} \cdot Q \right\rfloor,
\]

so that $N_{\text{persist}}(0) = 0$, $N_{\text{persist}}(5) = Q$, and
each burst adds approximately $Q / M = 12$ new particles to the ring.

\subsubsection{Placement and Kinematics}

Each persistent particle $i$ is assigned a fixed angular home position
$\phi_i$ uniformly distributed around the full circumference:

\[
\phi_i = \frac{2\pi i}{Q}, \quad i = 0, \dots, Q-1.
\]

The particle orbits at a radial distance $R_{\text{ring}}$ from the
circle centre, where

\[
R_{\text{ring}} = R_{\text{circle}} + \Delta R, \quad \Delta R = 28\ \text{px},
\]

placing the ring just outside the breathing circle's outer stroke.

Rather than remaining static, each active particle executes a gentle
\textit{orbital drift}:

\begin{align*}
  \theta_i(t) &= \phi_i + \omega_i \cdot t + A_i \cdot \sin(\omega_{\text{sway}} \cdot t + \psi_i), \\
  r_i(t) &= R_{\text{ring}} + B_i \cdot \sin(\omega_{\text{radial}} \cdot t + \xi_i),
\end{align*}

where
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\omega_i \sim \mathcal{U}(0.05, 0.20)$ rad/s — slow tangential drift,
  \item $A_i \sim \mathcal{U}(0.03, 0.10)$ rad — small azimuthal sway,
  \item $B_i \sim \mathcal{U}(2, 8)$ px — radial breathing,
  \item $\omega_{\text{sway}}, \omega_{\text{radial}} \sim \mathcal{U}(0.3, 0.7)$ rad/s,
  \item $\psi_i, \xi_i \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 2\pi)$ — random phase offsets.
\end{itemize}

These dynamics keep the particles perceptibly alive without distracting
from the primary breathing cue; the motion amplitude is deliberately
small (sub-pixel to a few pixels per frame at 60~fps).

\subsubsection{Stage Transitions}

When a burst fires and the stage increments from $s$ to $s+1$, the
newly activated particles do not appear instantaneously. Instead they
\textit{fade in} over $T_{\text{fade}} = 1.2$~seconds, with opacity
$\alpha_i(t)$ ramped from 0 to the nominal value $\alpha_0 = 0.75$
via an ease-out curve:

\[
\alpha_i(t) = \alpha_0 \cdot \left(1 - (1 - p)^3\right), \quad
p = \min\!\left(1,\; \frac{t - t_{\text{trigger}}}{T_{\text{fade}}}\right).
\]

This staggered emergence creates a soft, organic growth of the ring
rather than a jarring pop-in.

\subsubsection{Rendering}

Active persistent particles are rendered as small luminous dots:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Size:} $d = 6$~px diameter.
  \item \textbf{Colour:} HSL hue matching the most recent burst palette
    ($H \sim \mathcal{U}(190, 270)$), with saturation 70\,\% and
    lightness $65\% + 15\% \cdot \sin(\omega_i t + \phi_i)$ for a subtle
    shimmer.
  \item \textbf{Glow:} 8~px \texttt{box-shadow} at 40\,\% opacity.
  \item \textbf{Inactive particles} (stages not yet reached) are not
    rendered at all — there is no placeholder or ``empty slot.''
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Rationale.}
A transient burst alone creates a ``what just happened?'' problem: if
the user blinks or looks away during the 2--3~second animation, the
reward is missed entirely. The persistent ring solves this by providing
a continuously visible record of session progress — the user can glance
at the circle at any time and immediately see how many resonance events
they have achieved. The five-stage build-up from empty to full leverages
the \textit{endowed progress effect}: visible accumulation toward a
known maximum ($M = 5$) motivates continued engagement, as each earned
particle cluster both rewards past performance and signals proximity to
completion. The small orbital motion prevents the ring from being
mistaken for a static UI decoration, while staying subtle enough to
avoid competing with the breathing pacing cue.
